I am an assistant professor in the Department of Anthropology at the University of Pittsburgh. As a bioarchaeologist, I work with human remains and the objects people made and used to understand health, healing, and care in the ancient Maya world.

My research began with the mouth. My dissertation at Brown University traced cultures of oral care in the Southern Maya Lowlands from 250 BCE to 1000 CE, and my earlier work examined dental practice and bone-object production at Piedras Negras, Guatemala. I also build open digital tools for bioarchaeological research, including Project TOMBS and the MesoBIO database.
